Part I. Plan

The 6-month churn

In the myth, poison rises before nectar. Same here: months 0 to 2 surface the hard truths, months 3 to 6 compound what survives. Every month ends in a gate. Fail a gate twice and you pivot, not push.

The one rule that makes 6 months possible

Charge money from the first working version. Paid users give honest signal, fund your tools, and are the single strongest line in any grant or investor pitch.

Six months on one page

Blue: product. Gold: money and legal. Green: growth. Red: decision gates.

M0Weeks 0 to 3

Validate the problem, not the idea

  • 20 to 30 problem interviews. Ask about the last time the pain happened and what they paid to fix it. Never pitch in these calls.
  • One-page landing site with a price on it. Collect waitlist sign-ups and, ideally, refundable pre-orders or LOIs.
  • Sign the founders' agreement before anyone writes code. Equity, vesting, roles, IP, exit. See Co-founders.
  • Run name, trademark and domain checks (Day 0).

Real exampleDropbox validated demand with a short demo video and a waitlist before the product was broadly available (Case studies).

Gate: at least 5 people say yes to a price, or 3 pre-pay. If not, change the segment or problem, not the pitch.

M1Weeks 4 to 8

Form the company, build the thinnest MVP

  • Incorporate a Private Limited company via SPICe+ (7 to 15 working days). Open current account, appoint auditor within 30 days.
  • Apply for DPIIT recognition right after incorporation. It unlocks almost every scheme.
  • File trademark for name and logo (startup fee applies).
  • Build one core workflow end to end. AI coding tools make 3 to 4 week MVPs realistic for a strong engineer.
  • Line up 5 design partners who agreed to use it weekly.

Real exampleStripe's founders set the product up for early users in person. Do the same for your 5 design partners.

Gate: a design partner completes the core job without you on the call.

M2Weeks 9 to 13

Ship to real users and charge

  • Paid pilots, even small (₹999 or a monthly retainer). Discounts, not free.
  • Instrument activation, retention and time-to-value from day one.
  • Register GST when required or when B2B buyers need your GSTIN.
  • Stack cloud and tool credits, apply to 2 or 3 incubators.
  • Start founder-led content: one useful post per weekday.

Gate: 40% of active users would be "very disappointed" without it, or week-4 retention is flattening, not falling to zero.

M3Weeks 14 to 17

Prove it, then launch loudly

  • Public launch week (Marketing): one launch per day across channels.
  • Turn the 3 best customers into case studies with numbers.
  • Apply to grants and state programmes with that traction (Govt schemes).
  • Fix the top 3 reasons people drop off. Ignore feature requests from non-payers.

Gate: 10+ paying customers or a repeatable weekly sign-up curve.

M4Weeks 18 to 22

Scale exactly one channel

  • Pick the channel that produced the cheapest paying customer and double effort there. Park the rest.
  • First helpers: interns, freelancers or a part-timer on contracts with IP assignment. Process in Hiring.
  • Board-approve an ESOP pool (10 to 15%) before offering anyone equity.
  • Tighten compliance: TDS, GST returns, board minutes. Set up the controls in Finance function.

Gate: customer acquisition cost recovered within 3 to 6 months of revenue from that customer.

M5Weeks 23 to 25

Fuel: raise, or compound without raising

  • Build the data room and a 10-slide deck anchored on real metrics.
  • Talk to 30+ angels and micro VCs in a 3 to 4 week burst. Parallel, not serial.
  • If not raising: annual prepaid plans, services add-ons, revenue-based financing once revenue is steady.

Gate: either a term sheet in motion or 3+ months of runway from revenue.

M6Week 26

Decide with data

  • Scale: growth above 15% month on month and retention holding.
  • Raise: investors engaged and money buys faster growth, not survival.
  • Pivot: users love one piece but not the whole. Rebuild around that piece.
  • Stop: no gate passed twice in a row. Close cleanly (exit kit in Risks).

Roadmap variants

Same six gates, different shapes. Pick the variant closest to your model. Targets are month-6 benchmarks for a two-founder, near-zero-capital team.

Shape

Narrow vertical workflow, sold to teams who already pay for a worse tool or for manual labour. Price in INR for India, USD for global.

M6 target: 20 to 50 paying accounts, ₹1 to 3 lakh MRR, logo retention above 85%.

Moves that matter

  • Sell before building beyond the core workflow.
  • Founder-led sales to the first 20 customers.
  • Annual plans with a 15 to 20% discount fund the runway.
  • Integrations with what buyers already use (Tally, Zoho, WhatsApp, Google Workspace).

VishaHorizontal "AI for everyone" tools get cloned in weeks. Own a workflow, a dataset, or a distribution channel.

Shape

The engineer founder sells automation, integrations or consulting to fund the company, then productises the most repeated job.

M6 target: ₹2 to 5 lakh a month in service revenue, one productised offering with 5+ buyers.

Moves that matter

  • Fixed-scope, fixed-price packages, never open hourly.
  • Keep IP ownership of reusable code in every contract; client gets a licence.
  • Cap services at 50% of founder time by month 4 or the product never ships.

AmritServices profit and product losses sit in the same company, so one offsets the other for tax, and services can make you "default alive" by day 60.

Real exampleInfosys and Zoho both began by selling expertise and compounded that into products and institutions.

Shape

Fitness, social, career or creator apps. Retention decides everything; monetisation comes after a habit forms.

M6 target: 5k to 20k monthly actives in one niche, day-30 retention above 15%, first revenue from subscriptions or brand deals.

Moves that matter

  • Start with one tight community (one city, one college, one sport).
  • Seed with WhatsApp or Telegram groups you run yourself.
  • Weekly rituals: challenges, streaks, live sessions.
  • Ship Android first; most Indian users are there.

VishaPaid ads before retention is proven burns money fast. Fix the leaky bucket first.

Real exampleMeesho grew by riding WhatsApp groups that resellers already ran, not ads.

Shape

Two-sided: supply and demand. Liquidity in one small area beats thin coverage everywhere.

M6 target: in one city or niche, 30%+ of listings get a transaction every month; repeat buyers above 25%.

Moves that matter

  • Do the supply side manually first (concierge).
  • Take payment on-platform early to prevent leakage.
  • Check if you are an "e-commerce operator" under GST (TCS collection) and Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules.

Shape

Hardware, EDA, biotech, space, defence, novel algorithms with physical effect. Slower revenue, more grants, IP matters.

M6 target: working prototype, provisional patent filed, 2 paid pilots or LOIs, 2+ grant applications in review.

Moves that matter

  • File a provisional patent before any public demo or paper.
  • Apply for DPIIT Deep Tech recognition (20-year window, ₹300 crore turnover cap).
  • Grants: BIRAC, NIDHI-PRAYAS, iDEX, TDB, state deep-tech funds.
  • Sell a paid pilot or feasibility study to fund development.

Shape

Serving Bharat through NGOs, state bodies, CSR budgets or low-price B2C. Long sales cycles, large scale once in.

M6 target: 1 to 2 funded pilots (CSR, state or NGO), measurable outcome data, 1 impact investor or foundation conversation.

Moves that matter

  • CSR money flows through Section 8 or approved incubators; partner with one.
  • Vernacular voice-first UX.
  • Publish outcome data; it is your sales deck.
  • Use GeM (Government e-Marketplace) where startups get relaxed prior-experience norms.

Co-founders, equity, ESOPs

Co-founder conflict is among the most common reasons early startups die. Write the hard conversations down while you still like each other.

Equity split calculator

Score each founder 0 to 10 per factor. Weights reflect what drives value in the first 3 years. It is a starting point for the conversation, not a verdict.

Non-negotiables in the founders' agreement

Vesting

4 years, 1-year cliff, monthly after. In India, founders hold shares from day one, so vesting works as reverse vesting: the SHA lets the company or other founders buy unvested shares at face value when someone leaves.

Leaver terms

Good leaver (health, mutual exit): keeps vested shares. Bad leaver (fraud, breach, joining a competitor): vested shares bought back at face value or a discount.

Decision rights

Name who decides what: product, hiring, spending above ₹X, fundraising. CEO gets a casting vote on operations; equity and fundraise decisions stay unanimous.

Deadlock

48-hour cooling period, then a named mentor as mediator, then a buy-sell clause as last resort. Avoid a pure 50/50 with no tie-breaker.

Commitment

Full-time start dates, allowed side income, salary deferral terms. Mismatched commitment is the most common trigger of a split.

Restrictions

Post-exit non-competes are generally unenforceable in India (Section 27, Contract Act). Confidentiality and non-solicitation of staff and customers are the protections that hold.

ESOPs and dilution

  • Pool of 10 to 15% before seed; investors will ask for it anyway, better on your terms.
  • Minimum 1-year vesting is mandatory under the Companies Act rules. Board resolution plus shareholders' special resolution.
  • DPIIT startups may grant ESOPs to promoter-directors within 10 years of incorporation, which other companies cannot.
  • Tax: perquisite at exercise, capital gains at sale. For eligible startups, employees can defer the exercise tax until the earliest of 48 months, sale, or leaving. Verify section numbering under the Income-tax Act 2025.
  • Sweat equity lets you pay advisors or early staff in shares for know-how; startups get relaxed limits.

Illustrative: pre-seed sells 10% and creates a 10% pool; seed sells about 20% and keeps the pool at 10%; Series A sells about 20% with the pool at 12%. Real terms vary with traction and market.

Part III. Team

Hiring the first 10

Your first ten people set the culture that the next hundred copy. Hire slowly for fit, pay fairly, and move fast once you are sure.

Hire when

  • A founder spends over half their week on a repeatable task someone else could own.
  • Revenue or runway covers 6 months of that salary.
  • You can write the role's 90-day outcome in two sentences.

Do not hire when

  • You are hoping a hire will find product-market fit for you.
  • The work is a one-off (use a freelancer).
  • You cannot give them a clear owner and weekly check-in.

Employee, contractor or intern?

  • Employee: ongoing core work, equity-worthy.
  • Contractor: defined deliverable, independent schedule.
  • Intern: learning role, fixed term, stipend, mentor assigned.

Process: 10 working days, end to end

  1. Role doc with 90-day outcome, salary band, ESOP range. Post it publicly; it doubles as marketing.
  2. 20-minute screen by a founder: motivation, notice period, salary expectation.
  3. Paid work trial of 4 to 8 hours on a real, scoped problem. Pay for it; it attracts better people and protects your IP.
  4. Deep interview walking through their trial and one past project.
  5. Values conversation with the other founder.
  6. Two backchannel references from people they did not list.
  7. Offer within 48 hours of the last interview.

India-specific realities

  • VishaNotice periods of 60 to 90 days are common and offer drop-outs are frequent. Keep the second-best candidate warm and stay in weekly contact until joining day.
  • AmritTier-2 and tier-3 engineering colleges have strong students ignored by big recruiters. Placement cells respond to startups that visit in person.
  • AmritOpen-source contributors to your stack are pre-vetted: you can read their code before the first call.
  • Explain ESOPs in rupees: "At a ₹100 crore exit, your vested options are worth about ₹X". Most candidates have never been shown the maths.
  • Every offer letter carries IP assignment, confidentiality, and notice terms before day 1.

The one-page MIS

Cash

Balance, monthly burn, runway in months, receivables older than 30 days.

Revenue

MRR or monthly revenue, growth %, new vs expansion vs churned.

Unit economics

Gross margin, CAC, CAC payback in months, revenue retention.

Healthy direction of travel, not rules: SaaS gross margin 70 to 85%, CAC payback under 12 months; D2C contribution margin positive after marketing by the second order; marketplaces take rate stable while repeat rate rises.

Hidden costs founders forget

  • Payment gateway fees of about 2% on every transaction, plus GST on those fees.
  • TDS on payments to contractors, rent and professionals, deposited monthly.
  • GST input credit lost when vendors do not file their returns.
  • Annual audit, ROC filing and director KYC costs.
  • Foreign exchange spread on USD income; compare bank rates against specialist remittance platforms.

AmritFounders who do the books themselves for the first 6 months understand their margins better than those who outsource on day one. Hand it over once you know what the numbers mean.

Policy stack

Handbook (5 pages max)

Working hours, remote rules, leave, expenses, devices, code of conduct, how decisions are made.

Leave

A simple, generous, written policy beats an unwritten one. Include sick leave, earned leave, holidays, and statutory maternity benefit (26 weeks for eligible employees).

Performance

Quarterly check-ins against written goals. One annual compensation review, never ad hoc raises for whoever complains loudest.

POSH

Policy, Internal Committee with an external member, and annual awareness session once you reach 10 employees.

Exit

Handover checklist, access revoked the same day, full and final settlement on time, exit interview.

Remote work

Written weekly updates, core overlap hours, one in-person meet-up per quarter if the team is distributed.

Statutory points to design around

  • Labour Codes (in force from November 2025): "wages" for PF and gratuity must be at least half of total pay, so salary structures with tiny basic pay no longer work. Verify with your payroll provider.
  • PF from 20 employees, ESI from 10 in notified areas, gratuity after 5 years (shorter for fixed-term staff under the new codes; Verify).
  • Shops and Establishments registration and professional tax per state.

Culture you can write down

  • Three to five values phrased as behaviours ("we write it down", "customers before comfort"), not adjectives.
  • Weekly all-hands: numbers, wins, one failure and what was learned.
  • Founders take feedback publicly and act on it visibly. Nothing builds trust faster.

Tax holiday strategy

Correction to a common belief

The startup tax holiday is 3 consecutive years of 100% profit deduction, chosen by you, inside the first 10 years from incorporation. It is not 2 years. From 1 April 2026 it lives in Section 140 of the Income-tax Act 2025 (formerly 80-IAC). Incorporation must be before 1 April 2030.

Two gates, not one

Gate 1: DPIIT recognition

Easy, free, fast. Over 2 lakh startups hold it.

Gate 2: IMB certificate

Separate application to the Inter-Ministerial Board proving real innovation, scalability and employment potential. Only a few thousand startups hold it. Apply once you have audited accounts and traction; strong evidence beats early filing.

VishaOther conditions: Pvt Ltd or LLP only, not formed by splitting an existing business, not more than 20% used plant and machinery, audited accounts, turnover under the cap in the claim year. Verify the turnover cap for the deduction itself; the DPIIT recognition cap rose to ₹200 crore in 2026, while several tax sources still show ₹100 crore for the holiday.

  • AmritEarly losses carry forward for 8 years. Eligible startups get relief from the usual rule that a 51% shareholding change wipes out old losses, as long as the original shareholders continue. Verify the exact condition under the new Act before each round.
  • VishaRelated-party deals that shift profit into holiday years can be recomputed by the tax officer. Keep transfer prices at market rates.

Patents and IP

Trademark first, always. Patent only when the invention is technical, visible to competitors once sold, and central to the business or the fundraise.

Patent if

  • It is a technical method or device with a measurable technical effect.
  • Competitors could copy it after seeing your product.
  • It supports licensing, a deep-tech raise or a government grant.

Do not patent

  • Software "per se", algorithms, business methods (excluded in India).
  • UI and branding (use design registration and trademarks).
  • Things you can keep secret, like a model's training recipe (trade secret).
Timing rule

File a provisional application before any public demo, paper, pitch video or open-source release of the invention. Public disclosure can destroy novelty, and many countries give no grace period. The provisional gives you 12 months to file the complete specification.

Smaller angel round, or a strategic investment from a customer or partner.
If it all goes sideways
Founders only, product on minimum cost, keep serving paying users until the numbers speak for themselves.
Money

Investor pressure or harsh terms

A term sheet with heavy liquidation preference, veto rights or founder re-vesting.

Solution
Negotiate on the terms that matter most; price matters less than control and preference.
Approach
Get a startup lawyer, compare with market-standard terms, trade valuation for cleaner terms if needed.
Expected outcome
1x non-participating preference, reasonable vetoes, fair vesting credit.
Failsafe
Keep one parallel investor conversation alive for leverage.
Backup plan
Take a smaller round now on clean terms.
If it all goes sideways
Walk away. Bad terms compound for every future round.
People

Co-founder conflict

Repeated arguments, silent disagreements, decisions reopened.

Solution
Separate the issue from the relationship and use the agreed process.
Approach
48-hour cooling rule, write both positions down, decide by the decision-rights matrix, review date on the calendar.
Expected outcome
Decision made within a week, relationship intact.
Failsafe
Named mentor mediates within 2 weeks.
Backup plan
Restructure roles so each founder owns a clear domain.
If it all goes sideways
Use the buy-sell and leaver clauses in the founders' agreement; separate cleanly, protect customers and staff.
People

Co-founder leaves

One founder wants out or stops showing up.

Solution
Execute the leaver terms calmly and transfer ownership of their work.
Approach
Classify good or bad leaver, buy back unvested shares at face value, hand over accounts, code and customer relationships within a week.
Expected outcome
Cap table cleaned, company continues without legal cloud.
Failsafe
Access and documentation that make any single person replaceable (bus factor 2).
Backup plan
Bring in a senior early hire with meaningful equity for the gap.
If it all goes sideways
If the remaining founder cannot continue alone, sell or wind down while the company still has value.
People

Roles stay open for months, offers are declined.

Solution
Compete on what big firms cannot: ownership, learning, equity, flexibility.
Approach
Public role doc, paid trials, tier-2 college outreach, open-source contributors, remote hiring beyond metros.

One strong hire within 6 to 8 weeks.
Failsafe
Senior freelancers on defined modules with IP assignment.
Backup plan
Reduce scope so the founding engineer plus AI tools can carry it longer.
If it all goes sideways
Partner with a small dev agency on a fixed-scope contract while you keep hiring.

Closing cleanly keeps your director record clean for the next venture.

Law and disputes

Most legal pain in the first two years comes from five places: contracts, unpaid invoices, employees, data and filings.

Contracts that protect you
  • Limit liability to fees paid in the last 12 months; exclude indirect losses.
  • Indemnity only for your own IP infringement and gross negligence.
  • Payment in 15 to 30 days, interest on late payment, right to suspend service.
  • Arbitration clause with a named seat in your city and a sole arbitrator.
  • You own your platform and reusable code; client owns their data and deliverables made only for them.
Getting paid
  • Udyam registration lets you claim payment within 45 days from buyers, with compound interest at 3 times the RBI bank rate on delay.
  • MSME Samadhaan portal for delayed-payment cases; buyers also lose tax deductions on unpaid MSME dues beyond the limit.
  • Escalation path: reminder, formal notice, Samadhaan, then arbitration or commercial court.
  • Cheque bounce is a criminal offence under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act; send notice within 30 days of the bounce.
People and employment
  • The four Labour Codes took effect in November 2025, replacing older labour laws. Verify state rules that implement them.
  • PF from 20 employees, ESI from 10 (in notified areas), gratuity after 5 years of service.
  • POSH Internal Committee once you have 10 or more employees.
  • Interns need written terms, a stipend record and IP assignment.
  • Use consultants for genuinely independent work only; misclassification creates PF and tax exposure.
Consumers and platforms
  • Consumer complaints can be filed online (e-Daakhil). Clear refund terms and fast responses keep you out of forums.
  • E-commerce entities must show seller details, grievance officer and return terms under the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules.
Directors' personal exposure
  • Non-filing of financial statements or annual returns for 3 years disqualifies directors for 5 years (Section 164).
  • Unpaid TDS and GST collected from customers can lead to personal liability and prosecution in serious cases.
  • Foreign money without FEMA filings (FC-GPR within 30 days) attracts compounding penalties.

Part VII. Learn

Case studies

Real companies, the move that mattered, and how to copy it with almost no money. Cautionary tales included; they teach faster.

Zerodha

Founded 2010, bootstrapped, no VC money

Move: undercut brokerage pricing radically, then built trust with free investor education (Varsity) instead of ads.

Lesson: profitable from early on, a price disruption plus trust content can beat funded rivals.

Copy it: publish the best free course on your buyer's job; price visibly simpler than incumbents.

Zoho

Started 1996 as AdventNet, never raised VC

Move: built dozens of business apps at Indian cost, sold globally, opened rural offices such as Tenkasi.

Lesson: patience and cost discipline create a moat funded competitors cannot match.

Copy it: hire and train talent in smaller towns; price in USD abroad.

Freshworks

Chennai, 2010; Nasdaq listing 2021

Move: the founder spotted a Hacker News thread about a big rival's price hike and built a cheaper, simpler helpdesk.

Lesson: incumbents' price increases are market openings.

Copy it: track complaints about incumbents on forums and review sites; build for the unhappy.

Postman

Side project 2012, company 2014

Move: a free Chrome extension solving the founder's own API testing pain spread through developers.

Lesson: a free tool for your own problem can be a distribution engine.

Copy it: release the internal tool you wish existed; charge for team features later.

BrowserStack

Founded 2011, bootstrapped for years before first funding in 2018

Move: solved one painful developer job (cross-browser testing) and grew through self-serve sign-ups.

Lesson: a narrow, painful problem sold self-serve can scale without a sales team.

Copy it: pick one job developers hate; free trial, card on file, done.

Flipkart

2007, books first; Walmart bought a majority stake in 2018

Move: cash on delivery solved Indians' distrust of online payment.

Lesson: the local trust barrier, not the technology, is often the real product.

Copy it: ask what makes your buyer hesitate to pay, and design that fear away.

Nykaa

Founded 2012 by Falguni Nayar at about 50; profitable at IPO in 2021

Move: content and trust in a category where fakes were common.

Lesson: starting later in life with deep domain knowledge is an advantage, not a handicap.

Copy it: use your professional years as the moat; sell trust where the market has none.

boAt

Founded 2016

Move: sold through Amazon and Flipkart first, built brand through youth culture and cricket rather than own stores.

Lesson: borrow existing distribution before building your own.

Copy it: list on marketplaces from day one; spend on brand where your buyer already spends time.

Meesho

Y Combinator 2016

Move: enabled home-based resellers to sell over WhatsApp to their own networks.

Lesson: build on the channel Bharat already uses daily.

Copy it: design your first version as a WhatsApp flow.

Razorpay

Y Combinator 2015

Move: founders struggled to accept online payments themselves and fixed onboarding for developers.

Lesson: your own painful setup experience is a product spec.

Copy it: write down every friction you hit while starting up; one of them is a business.

Zomato

Started 2008 as Foodiebay

Move: scanned restaurant menus and put them online; search traffic compounded.

Lesson: unglamorous data collection can create a durable moat.

Copy it: collect the information your market lacks, by hand if necessary.

Paytm

Demonetisation, November 2016

Move: was ready when cash vanished overnight and pushed QR payments to small shops.

Lesson: policy shocks create sudden demand for whoever is prepared.

Copy it: follow draft regulations and budget announcements; build for the rule before it lands.

Airbnb

2008

Move: sold novelty election-themed cereal boxes to fund the company when investors said no.

Lesson: resourcefulness is a signal investors later reward.

Copy it: find one creative, legal way to fund the next 3 months without asking permission.

Dropbox

2008

Move: a simple demo video grew the waitlist from thousands to tens of thousands overnight, before the product was widely available.

Lesson: you can validate demand before building the full product.

Copy it: a 2-minute screen recording plus a waitlist page, shared where your buyers hang out.

Stripe

2010

Move: founders installed the product on users' computers on the spot.

Lesson: doing things that do not scale wins the first customers.

Copy it: offer to set everything up yourself on a call.

Slack and Instagram

Pivots from a game (Glitch) and a check-in app (Burbn)

Move: noticed the one internal tool or feature people loved, and rebuilt around it.

Lesson: a pivot keeps the team and the insight; only the wrapper changes.

Copy it: list which feature your most active users actually use. That may be the company.

NVIDIA

Near-failure in the mid-1990s

Move: after its first chip missed the market, bet the company on the next one; its CEO later said it was about 30 days from going out of business.

Lesson: survival through the first failure is the entry ticket.

Copy it: keep enough runway for one full product mistake.

WhatsApp

About 55 employees at its 2014 acquisition

Move: ruthless focus on one job (reliable messaging) with a tiny engineering team.

Lesson: a small team with sharp focus can serve hundreds of millions.

Copy it: refuse features that do not serve the core job.

Infosys

1981, seed capital of ₹10,000

Move: seven founders, tiny capital, sold services to global clients and compounded for decades.

Lesson: services-first, globally sold, can build an institution.

Copy it: sell expertise abroad early; reinvest profit into capability.

Byju's

From India's most valued startup to insolvency proceedings in 2024

What went wrong: growth funded by aggressive sales, large debt-funded acquisitions and governance gaps.

Lesson: governance and unit economics catch up with every valuation.

Avoid it: board with real oversight, audited numbers on time, no sales practices you would hide.

GoMechanic

2023

What went wrong: founders admitted serious financial reporting errors while chasing growth.

Lesson: misreported numbers end companies faster than bad numbers.

Avoid it: one source of truth for metrics, reviewed by someone who is not selling them.

Dunzo

Well funded, operations collapsed around 2024

What went wrong: expensive quick-commerce race with thin margins and heavy cash burn.

Lesson: a better-funded race is not a strategy.

Avoid it: only enter capital races if you can outlast the richest competitor.
