Part one

Foundations

Safety, method and the daily path. Read this part fully before touching the fire.

I

The Seven Seals

Non-negotiable. The traditions kept these practices behind teachers for a reason.

1. Fire breaths never near water

Wim Hof, bhastrika, kapalabhati and long holds can cause sudden blackout without warning. Never in a bath, pool, sea, car, or while standing. Sit or lie on the floor.

2. Never gaze at the sun disc

Not at sunrise, not at sunset, not through clouds. Retinal burns from sun gazing are permanent and painless while they happen. The solar method here uses closed eyes.

3. Skip intense methods if

Pregnant, epileptic, heart disease, high blood pressure, glaucoma, recent surgery, hernia, or a history of stroke. Keep to nadi shodhana, bhramari and coherent breathing, and ask a doctor.

4. Protect the mind

With any history of psychosis, bipolar disorder, dissociation or severe panic, avoid kundalini work, long retention and mirror gazing. Calm practices only.

5. What rises must return

End every session by bringing awareness down to the navel, then eat, walk, or touch the earth. Raised energy that is never grounded is the root of most trouble.

6. Know the stop signs

Chest pain, fainting, several nights of insomnia, racing heart, uncontrollable heat or shaking, feeling unreal, visions or voices that continue outside practice, wild mood swings. Stop all fire breaths, retention and kundalini work at once. If it lasts more than a few days, see a doctor.

7. Sober, rested, empty stomach

No alcohol or drugs within a day of practice. No intense breathwork after a meal or when exhausted. No long fasting combined with breathwork, cold, or sleep loss.

How to read this book

Traditional claims are given as the traditions state them. Where research has measured something, it is marked. Where it has not, you get a way to test it. Advanced bandha and kumbhaka work is best refined with a qualified teacher. This is not medical advice.

The grounding sequence

Use after any intense session, or at the first stop sign.

  1. Open the eyes. Look around and name five ordinary objects out loud.
  2. Feel both feet pressing into the floor.
  3. Breathe normally. Make each exhale a little longer than the inhale.
  4. Drink water. Eat something warm and heavy if energy feels too high.
  5. Walk slowly for five minutes, barefoot on earth if possible.
  6. Resume ordinary activity before any further practice today.

The integration test

After any strong experience, answer honestly. Any "no" means slow down.

  • Can I sleep, work and talk to people normally?
  • Can I tolerate disagreement without feeling threatened?
  • Can I tell the experience apart from my interpretation of it?
  • Can I skip a day of practice without anxiety?
  • Am I becoming kinder and steadier, not stranger and more isolated?
II

The Method of Three Layers

The single discipline that keeps practice deep and the mind clear: never confuse an experience with its explanation.

LayerQuestionExample
ExperienceWhat actually happened?"Warmth moved up my back for about a minute during bhastrika."
InterpretationWhat does my tradition call it?"Hatha yoga would call this prana entering Sushumna."
ExplanationWhat could physiology or psychology say?"Fast breathing lowers carbon dioxide and changes circulation and sensation."
VerificationWhat can be measured or reproduced?"Does a fingertip thermometer show a change? Does it repeat?"

When anything unusual happens, write four sentences

  1. I experienced...
  2. My tradition would interpret this as...
  3. An ordinary explanation might be...
  4. I currently have evidence for...

This lets you take profound experiences seriously without having to believe every interpretation.

The six capacities every tradition builds first

  • Attention. Placing and holding the mind where you choose.
  • Interoception. Feeling heartbeat, breath, heat, pressure and tension from inside.
  • Regulation. Feeling an emotion fully without obeying or feeding it.
  • Metacognition. Seeing a thought as an event, not a command or a prophecy.
  • Ethics. Power without restraint only amplifies old habits.
  • Consistency. A modest daily practice beats rare heroic sessions.

The 7-minute attention test

Take it on Day 1, 20 and 40. Choose one object, such as the breath at the nostrils. Set a 7-minute timer in the Chamber. Every time you notice the mind has wandered, make a tally mark and return without judgment. Record the tally count and a 1 to 10 clarity score in the Journal.

Prepare, practice, observe, record, question, test, integrate, repeat.

Weekly and monthly rhythms

  • Weekly: half a day of silence, a screen-free evening, one long sitting of 60 to 90 minutes, and the weekly review questions in the Journal.
  • Ekadashi: the eleventh lunar day, traditionally a light day of fruit and milk. Only if healthy, and never with fire breaths.
  • New moon: set one intention for the coming month.
  • Full moon: moon tratak and a release of what is finished.

Where they meet

Breath is spirit

Prana, qi, pneuma, ruach, spiritus: in Sanskrit, Chinese, Greek, Hebrew and Latin, the word for breath also means life or spirit.

The spine is the axis

Sushumna in India, the governing vessel in China, the djed pillar in Egypt, read as the backbone of Osiris.

Energy is refined, not spent

Brahmacharya, conserving jing, Hermetic transmutation. Less leakage through lust, rage, gossip and noise.

The heart is the judge

The Egyptian heart weighed against a feather. Anahata. The Taoist heart that houses the spirit.

Powers are a byproduct

Patanjali calls siddhis obstacles to liberation. The Buddha refused to perform wonders for crowds.

But do not force equivalence

When two systems both have a serpent, a fire or a center, ask: shared history, independent invention, similar function, modern reinterpretation, or surface resemblance? Prana is not automatically qi.

Practices from the other temples

Taoist

Zhan zhuang, standing like a tree

  1. Feet shoulder-width apart, knees soft, tailbone gently dropped.
  2. Arms raised in a circle at chest height, as if hugging a large tree.
  3. Crown lifted as if hung by a thread. Tongue on the palate.
  4. Breathe into the lower belly. Stay 5 minutes, building to 20.

Rooted below, suspended above, empty in the middle.

Hermetic

Thought observation

  1. Sit still with a timer set to 5 minutes.
  2. Watch thoughts like a cat watching a mouse hole, without joining them.
  3. Each time you get carried away, make one tally mark.
  4. Record the tally. Watch it fall over weeks.

The thought is an object. I am the one watching it.

Hermetic

Elemental breathing

  1. Choose an element: fire for drive, water for calm, air for lightness, earth for stability.
  2. Inhale and imagine its quality filling the body through every pore.
  3. Exhale and let the quality remain.
  4. 21 breaths, then 3 breaths of ordinary air to neutralize.
Egyptian, adapted

Uraeus gaze

  1. Eyes closed, awareness at the brow.
  2. Picture a cobra rising from the base of the spine and settling at the brow, hood open, perfectly still.
  3. Hold it as one-pointed, watchful attention for 5 minutes.

A modern adaptation of the uraeus symbol.

Egyptian, adapted

Ankh breath

  1. Picture an ankh at the heart: a vertical line with a loop on top.
  2. Inhale up the vertical line along the spine to the crown.
  3. Pause at the top of the loop.
  4. Exhale down around the loop and back into the body.
  5. 7, 14 or 21 cycles.

A modern visualization using an ancient symbol of life.

Egyptian

Weighing of the heart

  1. Sit in stillness. Picture a hall with a balance: your heart on one pan, a single feather on the other.
  2. Let anything heavy appear: a lie, a harm, a secret, a grudge.
  3. Name each one honestly to yourself.
  4. Release it with a long exhale and decide on one amend.
  5. Stay until the pans feel level.
V

The Serpent: Naga Shakti and Kundalini

The coiled power at the root, the dragon of the orbit, and how the traditions say to raise it safely.

What the tradition says

Kundalini, the coiled one, is Shakti, the creative power of the cosmos, lying asleep in the body as a serpent wrapped three and a half times around the self-born linga at the base of the spine. Her mouth covers the entrance to the central channel, Sushumna. Energy flows through the lunar and solar channels, and awareness faces outward.

Awakening means the serpent uncoils and rises through Sushumna, pierces three knots and seven centers, and unites with Shiva, pure consciousness, at the crown.

The three and a half coils are read in several ways: the three gunas, or the three states of waking, dream and deep sleep, with the half coil as turiya, the fourth state beyond them.

Naga Shakti

Naga means serpent, and Naga Shakti is serpent power. In Indian lore, nagas guard waters, treasures and hidden knowledge. Shiva wears Vasuki at his throat. Vishnu rests on Shesha, the endless serpent. Naga Panchami honours them each monsoon.

The inner naga is Kundalini. The outer nagas are her images: latent power, cycles of time, and knowledge that bites the unprepared.

The Hatha key

The downward wind, apana, is drawn up by mula bandha. The upward wind, prana, is pressed down by jalandhara bandha. They meet in the navel fire, sealed by uddiyana. Their union there is said to wake the serpent and drive her into the central channel.

The safe ascent: five stages

Stage 1

Base

Sleep, ethics, nadi shodhana, bhramari, zhan zhuang, clean food. At least two weeks.

Advance when calm, sleeping well, and 1:2 breathing is easy.

Stage 2

Seals

Mula, uddiyana and jalandhara bandha learned separately. Ashwini mudra.

Advance when every lock holds without strain.

Stage 3

Channel

Spinal breath, bija ascent, microcosmic orbit.

Advance when awareness holds along the spine for 10 minutes.

Stage 4

Fire

Bhastrika with maha bandha, breath of fire, tummo.

Advance after 10 days with no stop signs.

Stage 5

Ascent

The full Serpent Ascent and samyama.

Stay here. Depth, not novelty.

Signs are not goals

Traditions describe heat along the spine, inner sound, inner light, spontaneous movement, spontaneous breath suspension and waves of bliss. Record them as experiences first. Chasing them tightens the Rudra knot.

Core serpent practices

Stage 3

Bija ascent

  1. Sit tall, jaw relaxed, eyes closed.
  2. Awareness at the base. Chant LAM three times, deep and slow, feeling vibration there.
  3. Move up: VAM at the sacrum, RAM at the navel, YAM at the heart, HAM at the throat, OM at the brow. Three times each, pitch rising slightly.
  4. At the crown, no sound. Sit in the silence for one minute.
  5. Descend silently back to the navel.

Each sound is a key turning in its lock, one by one.

Stage 3

Microcosmic orbit

  1. Tongue tip on the palate behind the teeth. This bridges the two vessels.
  2. Rest attention at the lower dantian, three finger-widths below the navel, inside.
  3. Inhale up the back: perineum, tailbone, the point opposite the navel, between the shoulder blades, base of skull, crown.
  4. Exhale down the front: brow, palate, throat, heart, solar plexus, navel, back to the dantian.
  5. 9 circuits, building to 36. If breath and attention will not sync, move attention alone.
  6. Close by circling the palms over the navel, 9 times each direction, storing the warmth there.

The dragon rises up the back and returns down the front. Nothing is left in the head.

Stage 5 only. Empty stomach. Seated on the floor.

The Serpent Ascent, full sequence, 25 minutes

  1. Seat. Cross-legged, spine erect, 1 minute of stillness. Hands in Prithvi mudra, 11 breaths feeling the weight of the base.
  2. Balance. Nadi shodhana, 3 minutes, ratio 1:2.
  3. Wake the base. Ashwini mudra: 50 rhythmic contractions of the anal sphincter, breathing freely.
  4. Spinal breath. Tongue to palate. Slow ujjayi inhale while awareness travels up a thin silver thread inside the spine from base to brow. Pause at the brow. Exhale, awareness travels back down. 21 rounds.
  5. Stoke. Bhastrika, 20 breaths. Exhale fully. Hold out with maha bandha: chin down, abdomen drawn up, pelvic floor lifted. Hold only while easy. Release root, belly, chin, then inhale. 3 rounds.
  1. Seeds. The bija ascent, LAM to OM.
  2. Seal the gaze. Shambhavi mudra with the tongue curled back along the palate (khechari, non-surgical), 3 minutes, awareness at the brow.
  3. Witness. Drop all technique. Sit 5 minutes as pure awareness. Note any phenomena and return to stillness.
  4. Descend. Exhale awareness down the front to the navel, 9 breaths. Palms on the navel.
  5. Ground. Apana mudra for 1 minute. Drink water. Walk barefoot or eat within the hour.

I am not raising a force. I am removing what blocks it, and letting it find its own way.

Any stage, and on rest days

Gentle root-to-crown contemplation

  1. Sit upright and watch ordinary breathing for a few minutes.
  2. Bring attention to the base of the spine without contracting anything.
  3. Picture a small point of warm light there.
  4. With each easy inhale, let awareness widen upward along the central axis.
  5. With each exhale, release unnecessary tension.
  6. End by returning attention to the feet and the floor.
Phase IV

Samyama, Patanjali's key

  1. Dharana, holding. Fix attention on one object, such as the heart center. Return each time it slips.
  2. Dhyana, flowing. The returns stop. Attention flows to the object unbroken, like oil poured from a jar.
  3. Samadhi, absorption. The sense of a meditator fades. Only the object shines.
  4. Inquiry. From stillness, ask the object one silent question. Do not seek an answer. Afterwards, write whatever arose.

The methods

VII

Mudras and Bandhas

Mudra means seal. Hand seals anchor attention. Eye, tongue and body seals and the bandha locks redirect breath and energy. Effects listed are the traditional claims.

A daily mudra sequence

  1. Anjali mudra, 1 minute, to open.
  2. Adi mudra before pranayama, 1 minute.
  3. Gyan or Chin mudra for meditation, 10 to 20 minutes.
  4. One purpose mudra as needed, such as Prana for fatigue or Prithvi for grounding, 10 minutes.
  5. Apana mudra after intense practice, 5 minutes, to close.
VIII

Tratak: The Steady Gaze

One of the six Hatha purifications. Gaze at a point until the eyes water, then hold the image inside. The eye stills, and the mind follows.

Rules for every method

  1. Remove contact lenses. Face soft, jaw loose, spine tall, object at eye level.
  2. Start with 1 minute of gaze. Add 1 minute per week. Beginners may blink gently. Never strain.
  3. After gazing, close the eyes and hold the afterimage at the brow center. When it fades, rest in the space where it was.
  4. Repeat the gaze and afterimage cycle up to 3 times.
  5. Finish by rubbing the palms warm and cupping them over the eyes for 30 seconds. Splash cool water.
  6. Stop with eye pain. Avoid flickering light with epilepsy. Never the sun disc.

IX

Meditation, Mantra and Nada

The attention engine that every other practice runs on.

Stage 1, 10 to 20 minutes

One-point attention

  1. Posture. Sit with the spine naturally upright, not rigid.
  2. Object. Choose one: breath at the nostrils, a small visual point, a mantra, or a neutral sensation.
  3. Place. Rest attention directly on it.
  4. Detect. The moment you notice you have wandered, count it as a success. Noticing is the skill.
  5. Return. Come back without anger or commentary.
  6. Repeat. Wander, notice, return, thousands of times over weeks.
Stages 2 to 4

Beyond one point

  • Open monitoring. Let sounds, sensations, emotions and thoughts appear. Do not chase, do not suppress. Notice that each one changes. Rest as the awareness they appear in.
  • Self-inquiry. When a thought arises, ask: What exactly is being experienced? Where? What labels have I added? Can the observer itself be observed?
  • Absorption. Deepen one-point attention into samyama. Train stability first. Absorption arrives, it is not seized.

Measure progress by calm, honesty, kindness and less reactivity, not by unusual experiences.

  3. With a 108-bead mala, move one bead per repetition, turning back at the large bead rather than crossing it.
  4. When distracted, return to the sound.
  5. After the round, stop and sit in the silence it leaves.

Nada yoga

Listening to inner sound

  1. Sit for 5 to 10 minutes, ideally right after bhramari with shanmukhi mudra.
  2. Listen first to the farthest external sounds.
  3. Move to nearer sounds, then breath and body sounds.
  4. Rest attention on any faint inner ringing or humming. Do not strain to hear something special.

Hatha texts describe inner sounds shifting from loud, like ocean and drums, to subtle, like bells, flute and a humming bee. Record them as experiences before interpreting them.

Five minutes

Five-element meditation

  1. Earth. One minute sensing weight and contact with the ground.
  2. Water. One minute sensing saliva, pulse and fluid movement.
  3. Fire. One minute sensing warmth and digestion.
  4. Air. One minute sensing breath and every small movement.
  5. Space. One minute sensing the open space in which all of it appears.

The inner readings are a modern contemplative interpretation, not the classical meaning.

The five sources of siddhi

The opening sutra of the fourth chapter of the Yoga Sutras says powers arise from:

  1. Janma. Birth, the fruit of past practice.
  2. Aushadhi. Herbs and substances.
  3. Mantra. Sacred sound.
  4. Tapas. Austerity and disciplined heat.
  5. Samadhi. Absorption, the path this book follows.

The warnings inside the tradition

Patanjali states that these powers are obstacles to samadhi, though they look like achievements to the worldly mind.

In the Kevaddha Sutta, the Buddha refuses to display powers, saying the only wonder worth showing is instruction that frees the mind.

Never try to prove a siddhi through danger: no levitation stunts, fasting, sleep loss, breath deprivation, cold exposure or self-injury. Loss of consciousness is not attainment.

The honest ledger

Measured under controlled conditions

  • Inner heat. Tummo practitioners raised finger and toe temperature sharply (Benson, 1982). Vase breathing raised core temperature in Tibetan nuns (Kozhevnikov, 2013).
  • Stress and immune response. Wim Hof trainees released more adrenaline and had milder symptoms after an injected bacterial toxin (Kox, 2014).
  • Heart control. Slow breathing near 6 breaths a minute raises heart rate variability.
  • Breath. Trained freedivers hold their breath at rest for over 11 minutes.
  • Attention and pain. Long-term meditators show steadier attention and less distress from pain.

Not demonstrated

Levitation, invisibility, telepathy, remote viewing and precognition have not been shown in controlled, repeatable tests. Parapsychology results remain disputed, and a one-million-dollar prize for any paranormal ability under agreed conditions went unclaimed for decades.

That does not forbid testing. It means the test must be fair, or it will only show you what you hope to see. A failed personal test also does not refute a whole tradition. Match the conclusion to the experiment you actually ran.

The evidence ladder

LevelWhat you haveWhat it establishes
1One striking coincidenceAn interesting anecdote
2Repeated success without controlsInteresting, but wide open to bias

4Replication by you on fresh trialsStronger evidence
5Independent replication under skeptical controlsA strong empirical case

How to prove a siddhi

  1. Write a claim that can fail. "I can sense which of 5 symbols is in a sealed envelope better than chance."
  2. Set chance. 5 options means 20 percent by pure guessing.
  3. Pre-register. Before starting, write the trial count, what counts as a hit, and the pass mark. Date it and send it to someone.
  4. Randomize and blind. Another person picks targets with a random number generator, seals them, and leaves the room.
  5. Close leaks. No sounds, reflections, handwriting marks or body language that could give the answer.
  6. Record every trial. No dropping bad days, no stopping when ahead.
  7. Calculate. 100 trials at 20 percent: 28 hits passes at p under 0.05, 31 hits at p under 0.01.
  8. Replicate. Pass again with fresh trials, ideally run by someone else.

Remembering hits and forgetting misses, reading meaning into vague results, subtle cues from a helper, and changing the rules afterwards will fake any power.

The 90-second induction

  1. Choose one state.
  2. Take its posture.
  3. Say its one sentence internally.
  4. Picture a scene that carries it.
  5. Let it rise rather than forcing it. Notice what changes in the body.
  6. Release the image and return to neutral.

Laws of the keys

  1. Evoke, do not become. 90 seconds, then three slow breaths back to neutral.
  2. Never evoke rage, terror or hatred to gain power. What you rehearse, the nervous system learns.
  3. Fierce energy is used only in its protective form, and briefly.

XII

The Mind: Thought Process

What to hold in mind during practice, in free time, and when emotion runs hot.

During practice

  • Sakshi. Be the witness. Sensations, lights and thoughts are objects passing through.
  • Seventy percent. The Taoists never use full effort. Strain blocks flow.
  • Abhyasa and vairagya. Steady practice with no clinging to results.
  • Noting. If phenomena come, note "seeing" or "feeling" and return to the breath.
  • Close with care. End as carefully as you began.

I am the container, not the contents. This is passing through me.

In free time

  • So-Ham. Hear "so" on the inhale and "ham" on the exhale while walking or waiting.
  • Hourly check. Am I breathing low? Where am I holding tension? What am I rehearsing in my head?
  • One-minute sits. Several a day, eyes open, just presence.
  • Conserve. Fewer words, less scrolling, less arguing, less gossip. Leaks drain the vessel.
  • Ma'at. Speak only what is true.

When emotion runs hot

  1. Stop. Stop speaking and typing. Feet on the ground.
  2. Sigh. Two physiological sighs.
  3. Name and locate. "Anger. Heat in the face and chest. Fast breath."
  4. Allow. "I don't need to suppress it or obey it." Do not feed the story for 90 seconds. Let the wave crest.
  5. Ask. "What does this energy want to become?"
  6. Transmute. Move to the other pole on the same scale, below. Then choose the action.

Dream practice

Recall and lucidity

  1. Keep a notebook by the bed. On waking, stay still for a few seconds.
  2. Recall the emotion first, then scenes, people, words. Write immediately.
  3. Mark recurring dream signs: places, people, impossible events.
  4. During the day, ask "Am I dreaming?" and check: read a line of text twice, look at your hands.
  5. As you fall asleep, repeat: "Next time I'm dreaming, I will know I'm dreaming," while imagining becoming lucid in a recent dream.

Tibetan dream yoga rests attention at the throat as sleep comes. Never trade away healthy sleep for dream work.

5 minutes, lying in bed

The nightly review

Joins the Pythagorean evening examination with the Egyptian weighing of the heart.

  1. Replay the day backwards, from now to waking, like a film in reverse.
  2. Ask: What did I do well? Where did I go wrong? What did I leave undone?
  3. Weigh the heart: Was I truthful? Did I cause needless harm? Where did ego distort what I saw? Did I waste my life force in anger or excess?
  4. Choose one action for tomorrow that restores balance. Say it once. Sleep.

Let my heart be light as the feather.

Food for the Practice

Ayurveda sorts food by its effect on the mind: sattvic clarifies, rajasic agitates, tamasic dulls. Your own journal is the final judge.

Fresh seasonal fruit, cooked vegetables and leafy greens, rice, millets, oats, mung dal and khichdi, sprouts, milk, ghee, fresh curd at midday, paneer, soaked almonds and walnuts, sesame and pumpkin seeds, dates, raisins, jaggery, raw honey, tulsi, fennel, cardamom, turmeric, ginger, cumin, coriander, coconut water, warm water.

Avoid

Tamasic

Stale and repeatedly reheated food, deep-fried food, alcohol, tobacco, recreational drugs, overeating, heavy meals late at night.

Mitahara, the yogic way of eating

  1. Fill the stomach half with food, a quarter with water, and leave a quarter empty, as the Hatha Yoga Pradipika advises.
  2. Eat when truly hungry, in calm surroundings, chewing slowly. Pause in gratitude before eating.
  3. Make lunch the largest meal, when digestive fire is strongest.
  4. Practice first thing in the morning, or 2 to 3 hours after a meal.
  5. Wait 30 minutes after intense pranayama before eating.
  6. Last meal at least 3 hours before sleep. Sip water between meals rather than with them.
  7. Get enough protein from dal, paneer, curd and nuts. Light days only if healthy, and never with fire breaths or cold.
